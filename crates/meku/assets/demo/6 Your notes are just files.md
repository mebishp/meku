# Your notes are just files

## What that means

Every note in Meku is a small file ending in `.md` inside your mekuto, which is an ordinary folder. This whole tutorial is one: the `Meku` folder in your Documents (or in your home folder, if you have no Documents). You can see the files in any file manager.

That has some good consequences:

- **They're yours.** No account, no export step, no company that has to stay in business.
- **Any app can open them.** Notes written here open in any text editor.
- **They're easy to back up.** Copy the folder, or let a tool like Dropbox or Syncthing do it.
- **They're fast.** There is nothing to load from the internet.

## What "markdown" is

Markdown is the name for typing styles as symbols: `#` for a heading, `**` for bold, `-` for a bullet. It's been around for twenty years, which is why so many apps understand it. You've been using it throughout this tutorial.

## How Meku treats your files

- It never renames, moves or rewrites a note unless you ask.
- If another app changes a file you have open, Meku tells you instead of overwriting it.
- Your notes appear as plain text, and Meku keeps a small `.meku` folder for your open tabs and settings.

## Start your own mekuto

Click the mekuto name at the top of the sidebar and choose **Open or create another mekuto**. Pick any folder, or make a new empty one in the dialog, and it becomes a mekuto. `Ctrl O` does the same. Then press `Ctrl T` and write your first note.

Meku remembers the mekutos you have used, so switching back is one click.
